I built this portfolio with Nuxt, Vue, TypeScript and Tailwind CSS, mainly to try something new. React and Next.js are the default choice for most web projects, and I wanted to practise my skills outside the usual stack. Tailwind CSS, together with the Nuxt UI library's accessible components, let me design a distinctive look with consistent theming across the whole site while writing very little custom CSS.

## What I wanted to achieve

- **Accessibility first.** Accessibility is a core part of web development, and of daily life, yet it is often ignored or treated as an afterthought. This portfolio wouldn't reflect my skills as a developer, or my values as a person, if accessibility weren't central to its design and build. Every text and background colour pair is checked against the contrast requirements of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2. Every component is built with semantic HTML, with ARIA added only where HTML alone isn't enough. These are just some of the steps I took to make the site usable by as many people as possible.
- **Few dependencies.** Fewer packages mean faster installs and less to keep up to date. Plugins and module bundlers have made development faster and more capable, but every dependency also affects a project's security, maintainability, performance and reliability. Keeping external libraries, frameworks and modules to a minimum is the simplest way to reduce those risks.
- **Three languages.** The site is available in English, Canadian French and Vietnamese, because these languages and cultures are an important part of my life. Building it in all three lets me practise my comprehension in each language, and taught me how complex it is to design a project for a global audience. I also just really enjoy learning languages and might add more.
